3. AI agents act on your Mac, under your control

Advisor runs AI agents locally on your Mac using your own AI provider account (Anthropic Claude or OpenAI Codex). Depending on your settings, those agents can:

  • search and fetch content from the web;
  • read from and search connectors (MCP servers) you have configured;
  • read non-sensitive files in your home folder — agents are blocked from credential and key locations such as ~/.ssh, ~/.aws, ~/.gnupg and your keychains, and cannot write to or delete anything outside their own working state;
  • perform connector actions (send / create / update / …) — but only if you enable “Allow connector actions” in Preferences, which is off by default.

On the Codex backend two of those bounds are enforced differently and one is weaker. Advisor runs the Codex CLI inside a macOS sandbox that applies the same blocked locations and the same write restriction to the CLI and to everything it starts, including the read-only shell commands the Codex agent can run (the Claude backend has no shell). However, connectors configured in your own ~/.codex/config.toml are not gated by the “Allow connector actions” preference, and the Codex agent can read its own stored provider credentials in ~/.codex, which must stay readable for it to sign in. If either matters to you, use the Claude backend, which applies every restriction in-process and per tool call.

You decide which connectors Advisor can reach and which permissions are enabled, and you are responsible for those choices and for the consequences of actions agents take within the permissions you have granted. Advisor’s sandboxing of agents is best-effort, not a guarantee: AI systems can behave unpredictably, so do not attach connectors or enable actions whose misuse you are not prepared to accept.
