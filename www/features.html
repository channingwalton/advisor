Listening

Advisor hears both sides of a conversation: you through the microphone, everyone else through your Mac's audio. Nothing joins the call, so it works with any meeting app, a video, or people in the room.

  • Listen

    Press Listen to start. A red indicator and a timer show while Advisor is capturing. It never starts on its own.

  • Both sides

    The microphone and system audio (through ScreenCaptureKit) are captured together, so Advisor hears you and whatever is playing: Zoom, Meet, Teams, a video or a webinar.

  • Mute

    Mute your microphone during a session while system audio carries on being transcribed. Tap again to unmute.

  • Echo removal

    On a call, your voice comes back through the speakers into the microphone. Advisor removes that echo so you aren't transcribed twice.

Transcription

Speech is transcribed on your Mac by Apple's SpeechAnalyzer. There's no cloud transcription service and no model to download.

  • Live transcript

    Words appear in grey as they're spoken and settle into finished sentences.

  • Audio stays on your Mac

    Only transcript text is sent anywhere, and only to the AI provider you've signed in with.

  • No recordings

    No audio file is written. The transcript, saved as Markdown, is the only record of the conversation.

  • Language

    Choose the speech-recognition language in Settings, or leave it on System default to follow your Mac.

Cards

As the conversation goes on, Advisor decides what's worth looking up and adds a card to the transcript at the point it relates to.

  • Answer

    A question asked aloud, by you or anyone else, gets an answer.

  • Context

    Background on the people, projects, tickets, terms and technologies being discussed.

  • Fact-check

    Claims are checked against sources. When a claim can't be confirmed, the card says so rather than guessing. Fact-checks start collapsed.

  • Sources

    A short first answer appears, then a fuller one once research finishes. Every card cites its sources as links or file paths.

  • How often

    Choose conservative, balanced or eager, and turn each kind of card on or off. The Auto cards switch stops cards appearing by themselves while transcription carries on.

  • Speed

    A fast classifier decides whether to act in under a second, leaving the rest of the time for research. Research runs in parallel, so one slow question doesn't hold up the next.

Asking

Anything Advisor does by itself, you can also ask for, with the transcript as context.

  • Ask box

    Type a question at any time, about the conversation or anything else. The answer arrives as a card.

  • Right-click a line

    Select transcript text and choose Answer, Context or Fact check; the card appears under that line. The same menu copies text and corrects a misheard word.

  • Notes

    Right-click a line and choose Add note here… to add your own note to the transcript. Advisor trusts it over the speech recognition, may research it, and saves it with the session.

  • Follow-ups

    Ask a follow-up on a finished card and the reply is added to that card.

  • Card controls

    Hover a card to copy, collapse, expand or delete it. Deleting a card that's still researching stops the research.

  • Summary

    Summarise the whole conversation into a card in one click, live or on a saved session. The summary is saved with the session.

Recording window

One window that sits beside your call.

  • Float

    Keep the window above other apps so it stays in view during a meeting.

  • Stop and resume

    Stop pauses capture without ending the session, and Resume carries on with the same context. The session ends when you close the window.

  • Session context

    Tell Advisor who's there, what the topic is and what to focus on, before you start or during the session. Changes apply from the next card.

  • Name

    Rename the session from the toolbar while it records. A session started in a folder shows which folder it's saved to.

  • Cards only

    Hide the transcript to see only the cards, and tap again to bring it back.

  • Export

    Save a copy of the session's Markdown anywhere you like, during or after recording.

Sessions

The app opens on your past sessions, and you can keep asking them questions.

  • List

    Sessions are grouped by day, with search and a filter menu for this week and sort order. Each row shows the title, a one-line summary, the duration, the number of cards and the session's tags. Type #tag to filter by tag.

  • Detail

    The questions you asked and the transcript, with each card where it appeared, laid out exactly as in the recording window.

  • Same controls as recording

    Collapse, delete and follow up on cards, hide the transcript, and right-click a line to research it, add a note or correct a word. Every change is saved to the session's note.

  • Ask a past session

    Ask a question about a saved session. The answer is added to its note under Follow-ups. Stop a question or a summary while it runs and nothing is saved; a stopped question goes back in the field.

  • Rename, export, share, show and delete

    From the session's … menu, or by right-clicking it in the list. Share sends the Markdown file through Mail, AirDrop, Messages and the rest of the macOS share sheet.

Organisation

Folders, tags and saved contexts.

  • Folders

    Group sessions into folders. Each folder has its own record button, so a new session is saved straight into it. Move older sessions by dragging them onto a folder or with Move to Folder.

  • Folder context

    Give a folder a standing brief, such as the background to a weekly meeting. Sessions started there begin with it, and you can still edit it per session. Load fills it from one of your saved contexts.

  • Folder tags

    Give a folder default tags. New sessions started there get them, and you can change them per session.

  • Tags

    Create coloured tags and apply them from the recording window or the Sessions window. Renaming a tag updates it everywhere.

  • Saved contexts

    Keep the context for meetings you have often, such as a stand-up, a customer call or an interview, and pick it instead of retyping it.

  • Plain Markdown

    Each session is a Markdown file with its cards included, in a folder you choose. There's no database, so any notes app can open them.

Sources

Advisor uses what your AI provider can already reach. It has no accounts of its own.

  • Web search

    Built in, with nothing to set up.

  • Connectors

    Connectors your Claude or Codex setup already has, such as Jira, Confluence, Slack or your notes, are searched too. Add one to your provider and Advisor can use it.

  • Local files

    Read-only access to non-sensitive files in your home folder, so a card can cite the document being discussed.

Privacy

There are no Advisor accounts and no Advisor server. Audio stays on your Mac, and transcript text goes only to your own AI provider. Research agents are sandboxed differently for each provider.

  • On your Mac

    Transcription runs on-device, and Advisor only listens after you press Listen.

  • Claude

    Every tool call is checked against a policy that allows web search, connector reads and searches, and read-only access to your home folder. There's no shell and no file writing. Credential locations such as ~/.ssh, ~/.aws and keychains are blocked, and symlinks are resolved first so a link can't get round the list. The policy is checked before Claude Code's own permission rules, so allow rules in your Claude settings can't widen it.

  • Connector actions on Claude

    A connector call that isn't recognised as a read or a search counts as an action, and actions are blocked unless you turn on Allow connector actions in Settings.

  • Codex

    Codex has no per-call check, so Advisor runs it inside a macOS sandbox built from the same list of blocked locations. Codex, and anything it starts, can't read those locations or write files outside its own state and temporary folders. It's coarser than the Claude policy: Codex can run read-only shell commands and can read its own login in ~/.codex. It can't tell a connector read from an action, so with Allow connector actions off it gets no connectors, and with it on it gets them all.

Providers

Advisor runs on the AI subscription you already have. An API key is never required.

  • Claude or Codex

    All of Advisor's agents use one provider, Claude by default or Codex, chosen in Settings along with the model.

  • Your existing login

    Advisor uses your Claude Code or Codex CLI login, so usage counts against the plan you already have.

  • API key instead

    On a Mac without a CLI login, paste an Anthropic or OpenAI API key. It's stored in the macOS Keychain and checked with the provider when you enter it.

Settings

Everything is in one Settings window (⌘,).

  • Options

    The number of parallel agents, connector permissions, how often cards appear and which kinds, provider and model, card effort, saved contexts, tags, system-audio capture, transcription language, and where sessions are stored.

  • Effort

    How much Claude thinks before answering a card. Low is fastest and Max slowest; Default follows your own Claude Code setting. It covers cards, follow-ups, questions and summaries, and a level the model can't use is lowered to one it can. Claude only.

  • Appearance

    Light, dark, or follow the system.

Requirements

What Advisor needs, and one thing it can't do.

  • System

    macOS 26 or later on Apple Silicon.

  • Permissions

    Microphone, and screen and system audio recording, requested on first run.

  • AI provider

    A Claude Code or Codex CLI login, or an API key.

  • Install

    A signed, notarised app with its Node runtime bundled. There's nothing else to install.

  • Known limit

    When two people talk at once on the same audio, the transcript can't fully separate them. It recovers once one of them stops.
