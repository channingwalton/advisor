Feature guide

Everything Advisor does.

The complete tour — every feature, from the Listen button to the sandbox that keeps research on a leash. For the short version, head back to the overview.

01

Live listening

One button hears the whole room — you through the microphone, everyone else through the Mac's own audio. No bot joins the call, so it works with any app, any video, or a conversation across the table.

One button to listen

Press Listen and Advisor starts capturing; a pulsing red indicator and a running timer make it unmistakable that it's live. Listening is always explicitly user-initiated — the app never starts capturing on its own.

Both sides of any conversation

Advisor captures your microphone and your Mac's system audio (via ScreenCaptureKit) at the same time — so it hears you and whatever is playing: a Zoom call, a Meet, a Teams meeting, a video, a webinar. Nothing joins the meeting; nobody sees an extra participant.

Mic mute

Silence your own microphone mid-session while system audio keeps being captured and transcribed — useful when you want the room on record but not yourself. Tap again to unmute.

Echo dedup

On a call, your own voice comes back out of the speakers and into the mic. Advisor detects and removes that bleed, so you aren't transcribed twice.

02

On-device transcription

Speech becomes text on your Mac, using Apple's SpeechAnalyzer. No cloud transcription service, no audio uploads, no model files to download and manage.

A live transcript that firms up

Words appear in grey as they're spoken, then settle into finalised sentences — a clean, chronological feed of the conversation. Runs of transcript between cards are grouped into their own recessed panel, so speech and assistant output stay visually distinct.

Audio never leaves the Mac

Speech-to-text runs entirely on-device (macOS 26+). The only thing that ever goes anywhere is transcript text, and only to the AI provider you've signed in with.

Nothing is recorded

No audio file is ever written. The transcript is the only record of the conversation — stored as plain markdown you can read, move, or delete.

Locale-aware

Pick the speech-recognition locale in Preferences from the list Apple supports, or leave it on System default and Advisor follows your Mac's locale.

03

Proactive cards

As the conversation flows, Advisor decides what's worth helping with and slides a card into the feed, anchored to the exact moment it relates to.

ANSWERQuestions answered as they're asked

Ask something aloud — or hear someone else ask it — and an Answer card appears without anyone touching the keyboard.

CONTEXTBackground on what's in play

Context cards brief you on the people, projects, tickets, terms, and technologies being discussed — pulled from the web and whatever sources you've connected.

FACT-CHECKClaims checked against sources

Factual claims are verified against real sources — and when a claim can't be confirmed, the card says "couldn't verify" rather than guessing. Fact-checks start collapsed so they stay out of the way until you want them.

Cards stream in, then improve

A brief preliminary answer lands first, then a revised one replaces it as the research agent consults the web and your connected sources. Every card cites where it came from — links or file paths, inline.

Tunable triggers

Set the sensitivity — conservative, balanced, or eager — and toggle each card kind (answer / context / fact-check) on or off independently. An Auto-cards switch in the toolbar turns proactive help off entirely while you keep transcribing.

Built for speed

A fast classifier decides whether to act in under a second, leaving the research agent the rest of the budget to answer — so useful cards arrive within a few seconds, while the moment is still live. Research runs in parallel lanes, so one slow question never blocks the next.

04

Ask anything

The assistant doesn't just volunteer — everything it can do on its own, you can ask for directly, with the live transcript as context.

The query box

Type a question at any time — about the current conversation or anything else. The answer arrives as a card in the feed, with the transcript so far as its context.

Right-click any line

Select transcript text and run Answer, Context, or Fact check on it directly — the same actions the assistant triggers automatically — with the resulting card anchored under that line. The menu also copies the selection, and lets you correct a mis-heard word so the record is right.

Editorial notes

Right-click a line ▸ Add note here… to inject your own note into the transcript as a distinct NOTE card, anchored where you put it. The assistant treats your note as authoritative context — it can even trigger research of its own — and it's saved with the session, round-tripping through the markdown. Delete it any time.

Follow-ups turn cards into threads

Once a card is done, ask it a follow-up; the reply is appended in place, so a card becomes a short, focused thread rather than a scattering of new cards.

Card controls

Collapse, expand, copy, or dismiss any card. Dismissing a card that's still researching cancels its work — no orphaned agents grinding away in the background.

Summarise on demand

One tap distils the whole conversation into a Summary card — during the session or on a saved one afterwards — and the summary is kept with the note.

05

The recording window

A single window designed to sit beside your call — quiet when there's nothing to say, unmissable when there is.

Floating mode

Keep the window always-on-top so it stays visible next to your meeting without stealing focus from it.

Stop is a pause, not an ending

Stop pauses capture but keeps the session live; Resume picks it back up with full context intact. The session is only finalised when you close the window.

Session context

Tell the assistant who's present, what the topic is, and what to focus on — before you start, or edited live to steer it mid-conversation. Each change applies from the next card. Pick from saved contexts instead of retyping common setups.

Name it while it's live

A Name chip in the toolbar renames the session as it records; the Sessions sidebar updates immediately. Started from a folder, a read-only chip shows where the note is being written.

Cards-only view

Hide the transcript with one tap to watch just the cards stream in — the assistant's output and nothing else. Tap again to bring the words back.

Export

Copy the current session note to any location you choose, while it's still recording or after.

06

Sessions browser

The app opens on a master/detail browser of everything you've captured — and past sessions stay alive: you can keep asking them questions.

Grouped & searchable

Sessions are grouped by day — Today, Yesterday, Earlier this week, and so on — with search, an All / This week filter, and a recent / oldest sort toggle. Each row shows a title, a one-line gist, the duration, and how many cards were surfaced. Type #tag to filter by tag.

Full session detail

Title and metadata, the questions you asked, and the transcript rendered just like the live window — with every surfaced card inline, exactly where it appeared.

Ask-and-append

Ask a question about a past session; the answer streams in and is appended to that session's note under a Follow-ups heading. The saved transcript grows with the questions you ask of it.

Rename, export, reveal & delete

Give a session a short name for an easier-to-scan list, export a copy of its markdown, reveal the file in Finder, or delete it (behind a confirmation) — from the detail header's … menu or each row's right-click menu.

07

Organisation

Folders, tags, and saved contexts — light structure that pays off the more you record.

Folders

Group sessions into folders in the sidebar, children indented under their folder. Every folder header has its own record button — start a recording there and the note is written straight into that folder. Re-file existing sessions later with Move to Folder.

Folder context

A folder can carry a default context — say, the standing brief for a weekly meeting. Recordings started from that folder inherit it as the seed of their session prompt, still editable per session. The context travels with the folder through renames and storage moves.

Tags

A user-managed library of coloured tags. Apply them from the live toolbar or the browser; they show as coloured pills on session rows and are searchable. Rename a tag and it updates everywhere it's used.

Saved contexts

Pre-canned context prompts you can pick from instead of retyping common setups — one for the stand-up, one for customer calls, one for interviews.

Markdown, yours forever

Every session is a portable markdown file with its cards embedded inline, in a storage folder you choose. No database, no lock-in — your notes app can read them today.

08

Sources

Advisor researches across whatever you've already connected to your AI provider — it brings no accounts of its own.

Web search, built in

Cards draw on live web search out of the box — no setup required.

Your notes & work tools

Whatever connectors your AI provider is configured with — Jira, Confluence, Slack, your notes — Advisor can search them too, read-only by default. Connect a tool once for your provider and Advisor benefits automatically.

Local files

Read-only access to non-sensitive files in your home directory, so a card can cite the document you were actually talking about.

09

Privacy & safety

Privacy by architecture, not by policy: single user, no accounts, no server of ours — and research agents kept on a short, auditable leash.

Local-first

Transcription is on-device and listening is always explicitly user-initiated. To research, Advisor sends transcript text — never audio — to your own AI provider, on your own subscription.

Sandboxed research (Claude)

Every tool call the agent makes passes a per-call policy: web search, read/search-only connectors, and read-only home-directory access. No shell, no writes. Credential paths — ~/.ssh, ~/.aws, keychains and the like — are denied case-insensitively, with symlinks resolved first so a link can't slip past the denylist.

Sandboxed research (Codex)

The Codex SDK has no per-call hook, so the sandbox is coarser but stricter in kind: a process-wide read-only sandbox with approval set to never blocks all writes and escalation. Read-only shell is permitted there, and connectors come from your own ~/.codex/config.toml.

Connectors fail closed

Any connector call not recognised as a read or a search is treated as an action — and actions are blocked unless you've opted in with the "Allow connector actions" toggle.

Single user, no server

No accounts, no multi-tenancy, nothing of yours stored anywhere but your Mac. There is no Advisor server to trust, because there isn't one.

10

Providers & authentication

Bring your own brain. Advisor runs on the AI subscription you already pay for — it never requires an API key.

Claude or Codex

Every agent lane — the classifier, research, and queries — shares one backend: Claude (Agent SDK) by default, or Codex, picked in Preferences along with the model.

Your existing login

By default Advisor uses your Claude Code or Codex CLI sign-in — agent usage is billed to the subscription you already have, not by us.

Optional API-key fallback

For a machine with no CLI login, paste an Anthropic or OpenAI key instead. It's stored in the macOS Keychain — never in a config file — and validated against the provider the moment you enter it, so a bad key surfaces immediately.

11

Preferences & appearance

One Settings window (⌘,) holds every knob — and the whole UI follows your Mac's look.

Everything in Settings

Parallel agent count, connector permissions, trigger sensitivity and per-kind card toggles, agent backend and model, saved contexts, the tag library, system-audio capture, the speech-recognition locale, and where sessions are stored.

Light, dark, or system

The entire app — live window, cards, and Sessions browser — is themed for light and dark, or follows the system automatically.

12

Requirements & honesty

What it needs, and the one thing it can't do.

A single, self-contained app

The download is a signed, notarised app with everything bundled inside — including its Node runtime. Nothing else to install.

What it needs

macOS 26 or later on Apple Silicon; microphone and screen & system-audio permissions (granted on first run); and a Claude Code or Codex CLI login — or an API key — for the research side.

Known limit

When two people talk over each other on a single mixed mono feed, no single-stream transcriber can fully separate them — Advisor included. It recovers as soon as one voice yields.
